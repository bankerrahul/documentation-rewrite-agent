# How to Change the Lesson Type in Thrive Apprentice
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
In this article, you'll learn about the different lesson types available in Thrive Apprentice, how to change a lesson's type, and when to use each format for the best learning experience.
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
## Available Lesson Types
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
Thrive Apprentice offers three lesson types, each designed for a different content delivery style:
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
- **Text** — A written lesson built using the Thrive Architect editor. Use text lessons for detailed explanations, step-by-step tutorials, downloadable resources, or any content that benefits from a rich text format with images, buttons, and other visual elements.
- **Video** — A lesson centered around an embedded video. Use video lessons for demonstrations, walkthroughs, lectures, or any content that's easier to teach visually. You can still add supporting text below the video.
- **Audio** — A lesson built around an embedded audio file. Use audio lessons for podcasts, interviews, guided meditations, or any content where students can listen on the go without needing to watch a screen.
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
**Tip:** You're not locked into your initial choice. You can change a lesson's type at any time without losing the content you've already added. The existing content is preserved—only the lesson's primary media format changes.
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
## How to Change a Lesson Type
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
Follow these steps to switch a lesson from one type to another:
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} -->
1. Navigate to **Thrive Dashboard** > **Thrive Apprentice**.
2. Click **Courses** in the left sidebar and select the course that contains the lesson you want to modify.
3. Open the **Content** tab to see all modules, chapters, and lessons.
4. Hover over the lesson you want to change.
5. Click **Edit Details** from the options that appear.
6. In the lesson details panel, locate the **Lesson Type** setting.
7. Select the new type: **Text**, **Video**, or **Audio**.
8. If you selected **Video** or **Audio**, add the media source (e.g., a YouTube URL, Vimeo link, or uploaded file).
9. Click **Save** to apply the change.
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
The lesson will now display in the new format when students access it.
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
## When to Use Each Lesson Type
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
Choosing the right lesson type depends on your content and your audience's learning preferences. Here's a quick guide:
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
### Text Lessons
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
Best for:
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
- Detailed, reference-heavy content that students may want to search or skim.
- Step-by-step instructions with screenshots or diagrams.
- Lessons that include downloadable files, code snippets, or formatted tables.
- Content that needs frequent updates—text is the easiest format to edit.
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
### Video Lessons
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
Best for:
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
- Visual demonstrations such as software tutorials or physical processes.
- Lecture-style teaching where seeing the instructor builds connection.
- Complex concepts that are easier to explain with animations or screen recordings.
- Courses where engagement and completion rates are a priority—video tends to hold attention longer.
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
### Audio Lessons
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
Best for:
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
- Conversational content like interviews, discussions, or Q&A sessions.
- Material that students can consume while multitasking—commuting, exercising, or doing chores.
- Guided exercises such as meditations, language practice, or pronunciation drills.
- Supplementary content that complements a primary video or text lesson.
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
**Note:** You can mix lesson types within the same course. A well-structured course often combines all three formats to cater to different learning styles and keep students engaged.
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
